# The Game

A duel. Two duelists. Ten paces, and whatever's left after that.

This page is the canonical rulebook for **Pistols at Dawn** as it runs onchain today. If an older page contradicts this one, this one wins.

## Overview

A **duel** is a dispute between two duelists, settled by pistols and, if honour demands it, blades. Each duelist secretly locks in a plan — when to fire, when to dodge, how to carry themselves, and what they'll draw if it comes to blades — before either side knows what the other has chosen. The plan is revealed only once both sides are committed, so neither duelist can react to the other's choice. It can end in one shot, or run the full ten paces and spill into a blade fight. Someone walks away. Sometimes nobody does.

## The Duel

A duel begins when a duelist issues a **Challenge**, naming an opponent (or making an open call anyone can accept), a **premise** — the stated reason for the fight — and an expiry window (one day by default if none is set).

Premises are flavour text attached to the fight: Undefined, Matter, Debt, Dispute, Honour, Hatred, Blood, Nothing, Tournament, Treaty, Lesson. Duels created through matchmaking always carry the premise **Honour**.

A Challenge moves through these states:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Awaiting | Challenge issued, waiting for a reply |
| Withdrawn | The challenger pulled the challenge back |
| Refused | The challenged party declined |
| Expired | Nobody replied before the expiry window closed |
| InProgress | Accepted — both duelists are now committing/revealing moves |
| Resolved | The duel concluded with a win, a loss, or a mutual kill |
| Draw | The duel concluded with neither duelist standing (or a bloodbath) |

The challenger can only withdraw their own challenge — they cannot accept it. An **open** challenge (no named opponent) can be accepted by anyone; a **targeted** challenge can only be accepted by the named opponent. Accepting moves the Challenge to InProgress and starts the commit clock.

Duels come in several types: **Seasonal** (ranked PvP, requires at least one life staked), **Practice** (no lives staked), **Tutorial**, **BotPlayer** (against a house bot, no lives staked), and **Ranked**/**Unranked** (matchmaking-only queues).

## Committing Your Plan

Before a shot is fired, both duelists **commit** — they submit only a cryptographic hash of their plan, keeping the plan itself secret. A plan is four cards:

1. A **Fire** pace (1–10) — when you pull the trigger.
2. A **Dodge** pace (1–10, optional, and it must differ from your fire pace) — the one pace you're braced to evade.
3. A **Tactics** card — how you carry yourself into the fight.
4. A **Blades** card — your fallback if pistols don't settle it.

Once both duelists have committed their hash, the round moves to **Reveal**: each side submits their actual four cards plus the secret salt used to hash them. The salt is checked against the original commitment before the moves are accepted, so nobody can change their mind after seeing the other side's hash.

The duel's randomness seed is built by combining **both duelists' salts together**. Since neither salt is known to the other side until reveal, and the seed doesn't exist until both have revealed, there is no way for either duelist — or anyone else — to predict how the dice will fall before the duel is fully committed.

## The Pistols Round

There is exactly one Round of combat per duel. Within it, the pistols phase steps through paces 1 to 10 in order. At the start of the phase, both duelists' **Tactics** cards apply, then both duelists' **Blades** cards apply their pre-shot bonuses — before a single shot is fired.

Each duelist starts a duel with:

- **Health**: 3
- **Base hit chance**: 50%
- **Base damage**: 1

At each pace, one **Environment** card is drawn from a shared, shuffled deck and applied to whichever duelist(s) haven't fired yet. If a duelist's chosen fire pace matches the current pace, their shot resolves right there: chance is rolled, and a hit lands if the roll succeeds — unless the target's dodge pace happens to be exactly that same pace, in which case the shot fails to connect regardless of the roll. There's no separate "who shoots first" stat — if both duelists chose to fire on the same pace, both shots resolve together at that step.

The pistols phase ends the moment either duelist's health hits zero, or once both duelists have fired — whichever comes first.

Health floors at zero, and starting damage is 1, so a duelist facing no modifiers at all dies to three unblocked hits. Environment cards, Tactics cards, and the Blades card can push a single hit's damage well past 1, so a duelist stacking damage bonuses can kill in fewer hits.

> There is no fixed table of "chance by pace" in the current rules — pace only decides *when* your shot resolves and which Environment card lands on you first. Your actual odds on any given pace are whatever the base 50% has become after that pace's card, and any Tactics or Blades effects, are applied.

## Environment Cards

The **Environment deck** has 34 cards, shuffled fresh for every duel. One card is drawn per pace and applied to any duelist who hasn't yet fired — so the deck can hit both duelists in the same pace, or just one, depending on who's already shot.

| Card | Copies | Rarity | Effect |
|---|:---:|---|---|
| Damage Up | 7 | Common | +1 damage |
| Damage Down | 5 | Common | −1 damage |
| Chances Up | 7 | Common | +10% hit chance |
| Chances Down | 5 | Common | −10% hit chance |
| Double Damage Up | 3 | Uncommon | +2 damage |
| Double Chances Up | 3 | Uncommon | +20% hit chance |
| All Shots Hit | 1 | Special | Forces a hit; chance effectively maxed out, and suppresses further chance modifiers that pace |
| All Shots Miss | 1 | Special | Forces a miss |
| Double Tactics | 1 | Special | Doubles that duelist's Tactics-card effect (or cancels a prior No Tactics) |
| No Tactics | 1 | Special | Negates or reverses that duelist's Tactics-card effect |

That's 20 positive cards, 10 negative, and 4 specials — the deck leans in your favour two to one, but **All Shots Miss** only has to come up once.

## Tactics Cards

Every duelist picks exactly one **Tactics** card before the duel begins — there's no sitting this one out. Its effect applies once, at the start of the round, before the first pace.

| Card | Effect | Flavour |
|---|---|---|
| Insult | Opponent: −10% chance, +1 damage | Rattles their aim; sharpens their anger. |
| Coin Toss | Blocks the first Special-rarity Environment card drawn against you | Insurance against All Shots Miss or No Tactics. |
| Vengeful | +1 damage on your own shot | Straightforward. More powder, more pain. |
| Thick Coat | Opponent deals −1 damage to you | Their bullet hurts less. Sometimes not at all. |
| Reversal | The next decrease-effect that would hit you is negated and flips to an increase instead | A sudden change of fortune, right when you need it. |
| Bananas | Both duelists: −10% chance | Mutual sabotage. Nobody aims well on a banana. |

> **Insult** reads oddly on paper — it drops the opponent's hit chance *and* raises the damage they deal. Take it as written: sharpened aim doesn't come with it, but a sharpened blow does.

## Blades Round

If both duelists survive the pistols phase and at least one of them chose something other than **None** as their Blades card, the duel doesn't end there — it settles with a single, instant Blades resolution. There's no dice roll here; it's a straight lookup against the triangle below.

Every duelist also picks their Blades card before the duel starts, alongside Tactics — it isn't a separate late decision. Its **pre-shot** effect applies immediately, at the same time as Tactics, before pace 1. Its **clash** effect only matters if the duel actually reaches the Blades round.

| Card | Pre-shot effect | Beats | Clash outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| None | No bonus | — | Loses to any real Blades card |
| Seppuku | Self: +20% chance, +1 damage | — | Always dies, regardless of the opponent's card |
| Pocket Pistol | Opponent: −10% chance | Behead | Survives against Behead |
| Behead | Self: +1 damage | Grapple | Survives against Grapple |
| Grapple | Opponent: −1 damage | Pocket Pistol | Survives against Pocket Pistol |

The triangle: **Pocket Pistol** beats **Behead**, **Behead** beats **Grapple**, **Grapple** beats **Pocket Pistol**. Two identical cards — including Seppuku against Seppuku — is a **bloodbath**: both duelists' health drops to zero and the duel ends in a draw. Seppuku is the strongest pre-shot bonus in the game, but it carries a price: reach the Blades round holding it, and you die there no matter what your opponent drew.

## Winning and Losing

The winner is decided purely by who's still standing when the Round ends:

- **Win**: your health is above zero and your opponent's is zero.
- **Loss**: the reverse.
- **Draw**: both duelists' health is zero at the same time — whether from a simultaneous pistols kill or a Blades bloodbath.

The duel's **final blow** is recorded as either a specific Paces card (it ended during the pistols phase), a specific Blades card (it ended in the tiebreak), or **Forsaken**, meaning a duelist failed to commit or reveal in time and the duel was closed out without a shot fired.

## Honour and Archetypes

Every duelist carries a rolling **Honour** score from 0 to 100, and the game still buckets duelists into **Archetypes** — **Honourable** (70+), **Trickster** (40–69), or **Villainous** (below 40, once a duelist has fought at least one duel). But none of this feeds back into a duel's hit chances, damage, or outcome. It's a reputation layer, nothing more: your Honour for a given duel comes from which pace you chose to fire on (later paces read as more honourable), or jumps straight to 100 if you drew Seppuku. Bots carry archetype flavour in their profiles — TinMan plays Villainous, Scarecrow plays Trickster, Leon plays Honourable — and trophies still exist for honourable or villainous deaths. Treat Honour and Archetype as flavour and bragging rights, not mechanics.

## Duelists

A **Duelist** is an ERC-721 token — a character, not just an account. Each one carries a profile (Character, Bot, Genesis, Legends, or Pirates lineage), a lifetime record of duels fought, won, lost and drawn, and its own **FAME** balance.

**FAME and lives.** A duelist's lives are its FAME balance divided by 1,000 — a new duelist mints with 3,000 FAME, giving it exactly 3 lives. Lose a staked duel and you burn FAME equal to 1,000 FAME per life staked. Winning grants FAME too, but inversely: the richer in FAME a duelist already is, the less it gains per win.

**FOOLS.** This is the game's reward and wager currency, paid out to the winner of Season and Unranked duels (not Practice). Unlike FAME, FOOLS scales the other way — a wealthier duelist earns *more* FOOLS per win, scaled by lives staked.

**Signet Rings** are season-participation badges that boost the FOOLS you earn on a win: a Gold ring (earned by playing Season 1) gives +40%, a Silver ring (Seasons 2–4) gives +20%, a Lead ring (Seasons 5–9) gives +10%.

**Seasons** run on a leaderboard and points system, typically lasting two weeks. Winners score based on their kill pace, losers score a flat consolation amount, and a handful of bonus points apply for dodges and clean hits. When a season ends, the pool pays out to the top 10 positions on the leaderboard, weighted toward first place.

**Matchmaking** runs through two queues: **Unranked** (free to enter) and **Ranked** (5 FOOLS to enlist, and enlisting locks that duelist to ranked play for the rest of the season). Both queues stake exactly one life per duel and always fight under the Honour premise. Queues run at two speeds: Fast (10-minute commit window) or Slow (a full day).

**Packs** are consumable ERC-721 tokens that mint new duelists. A free **Starter Pack** hands every new player two duelists to begin with; beyond that, a **Genesis 5-pack** costs 50 LORDS and a **Pirates 5-pack** costs 100 LORDS.
