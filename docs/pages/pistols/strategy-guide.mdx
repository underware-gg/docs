# Strategy Guide

Pick your cards blind, then find out if you picked well. This guide breaks down the five decisions that decide a duel and gives you the numbers behind each one, so you can stop guessing and start reading your opponent instead.

It applies wherever you duel. The onchain game at [play.pistols.gg](https://play.pistols.gg/) and Classic duels through the [Discord Bot](/pistols/discord-bot) run the same core mechanics: 3 health, a flat 50% base hit chance, 1 base damage, the same 34-card Environment deck, the same six Tactics cards, and the same four Blades cards with the same triangle. What differs between them is what you stand to win or lose, not how you play. Nothing on this page changes because a duel is staked.

> The percentages come from a Monte Carlo simulation of 300,000 duels. Treat them as directional rather than exact, particularly the dodge figures: the simulation was run against a slightly earlier dodge model, so the shape of the advice holds but the decimal places shouldn't be trusted too hard.

## The Core Tension

Pistols at Dawn is a game of **social deduction meets strategic commitment**. You lock in your entire game plan blind, then watch it collide with your opponent's choices and the shuffle of the Environment deck.

There's no dominant strategy. Every strong play has a counter, every counter has a counter-counter. Reading your opponent matters as much as picking "the best" cards.

## The Five Key Decisions

### 1. When to fire

Fire pace is the single most important choice you make. It sets your initiative, your damage potential, and your odds of surviving to pull the trigger at all.

| Fire pace | Avg damage | Chance of lethal (≥3) | Chance you're dead first |
|-----------|:----------:|:----------------------:|:--------------------------:|
| 1  | 1.7 | 24% | 0%  |
| 3  | 2.2 | 40% | 3%  |
| 5  | 2.7 | 50% | 7%  |
| 7  | 3.0 | 58% | 13% |
| 10 | 3.6 | 65% | 24% |

**The tradeoff:** fire early and you're guaranteed to get your shot off; you can't be dead yet. But early shots rarely kill, since base damage is 1 and few Environment cards have stacked up. Fire late and your damage climbs with every card drawn, but if your opponent fires first and kills you, you never get to shoot at all.

**Rule of thumb:** without damage-boosting cards, firing early is often wasted. With them, firing early lets you cash in before your opponent gets the chance to respond.

### 2. Where to dodge

Guess right and you have done the single most powerful thing available to you. A dodge that lands on the exact pace your opponent fires negates the shot outright: not a reduced chance, not less damage, nothing at all. Their whole plan, the pace they picked and every card stacked behind it, spent on empty air. That's worth roughly +47.6% on your win rate, and no card in the game comes close.

Guess wrong and you've lost nothing. The dodge simply doesn't come up.

- **Correct dodge:** roughly +47.6% win rate
- **Wrong dodge:** no effect either way

So this is never a question of whether, only where; you dodge in every duel, and the only way to waste the choice is to put it on the pace you're already firing at, which you can't do. Guessing costs nothing and landing it can win the duel outright, which makes reading your opponent the highest-value skill in the game. Anyone who always fires at pace 1 is begging to be dodged at pace 1, right up until they notice you've noticed. That loop is the whole social deduction game.

### 3. Tactics card: offence vs. defence

Both players choose one of the six Tactics cards. They range from aggressive to defensive.

**Aggressive:**

- **Vengeful** (+1 damage to you): a straightforward damage boost that pairs best with early-to-mid fire paces, where the extra point can push you over the kill threshold.
- **Reversal** (flips the next negative event card to positive) has the highest kill rate of any Tactics card at 28.8%. It turns bad luck into good, but does nothing if your events are already positive.
- **Insult** (+1 damage to your opponent, −10% opponent hit chance) is a double-edged sword: the hit-chance penalty is strong, but you're gifting them extra damage if their shot lands.

**Defensive:**

- **Thick Coat** (−1 opponent damage) is pure defence: it cuts the chance their shot is lethal.
- **Bananas** (−10% hit chance to both) is mutual disruption, slightly favouring whoever cares less about landing a hit.

**Situational:**

- **Coin Toss** (blocks the first special Environment card you'd otherwise suffer) is insurance against the rare but devastating All Shots Hit, All Shots Miss, Double Tactics, or No Tactics draws: low average value, high ceiling when it fires.

### 4. Blades card: the other half of the game

Over 55% of duels reach the blades round. Your Blades card choice is often the deciding factor.

The triangle:

- **Pocket Pistol** beats Behead (pistol bonus: −10% opponent hit chance)
- **Behead** beats Grapple (pistol bonus: +1 damage to you)
- **Grapple** beats Pocket Pistol (pistol bonus: −1 opponent damage)
- **Seppuku** loses to everything (pistol bonus: +20% hit chance, +1 damage)

Pick the same Blades card as your opponent and **both of you lose**. That happens in roughly 14% of duels and accounts for most mutual-loss outcomes.

### 5. Character

Character choice doesn't affect gameplay anywhere, so pick whoever you like.

> The same holds onchain: which Duelist you field doesn't touch the resolution engine. Hit chance, damage, and the win condition are decided purely by Paces, Tactics, and Blades cards. Honour is a reputation score, not a modifier, and Signet Rings and season standing affect your FOOLS payout rather than your odds of winning. Nothing you own makes your shot land more often.

## Archetypes

**Archetype** is shorthand for when you shoot. The game tracks it, since honour is earned by the pace you fire at and your archetype follows from the honour you accumulate, but it buys you nothing: no bonus, no better odds. It's a label for a firing rhythm and the quickest way to describe how someone plays.

| Archetype | Fires at |
|---|---|
| Villainous | paces 1–3 |
| Trickster | paces 4–6 |
| Honourable | paces 7–10 |

The labels earn their keep as a read. Work out which rhythm your opponent favours and you've narrowed ten possible fire paces down to three or four, which is most of the work of placing a dodge. Duellists who always shoot early are Villainous whether they call themselves that or not, and they're the easiest to dodge.

## Playstyles

Four builds worth knowing, as starting points rather than solved answers.

### The Aggressive Rush

**Goal:** kill your opponent before they can respond.

- Fire at pace 1–3
- Dodge early too, around pace 1–3, where an opponent racing you is likely to fire
- Vengeful or Reversal for Tactics
- Behead or Seppuku for Blades (pistol damage bonus)

**Strengths:** maximum initiative. A lethal shot means your opponent never fires back.

**Weaknesses:** low damage at early paces means most shots are non-lethal. Miss the kill and your opponent gets a free shot at you with your own shot already spent.

### The Defensive Wall

**Goal:** survive your opponent's shot, then fire late with a lethal payload.

- Fire at pace 7–10
- Thick Coat for Tactics (−1 opponent damage)
- Grapple for Blades (−1 opponent damage, stacked with Thick Coat that's −2)
- Dodge at your best guess of their fire pace

**Strengths:** Thick Coat plus Grapple cut opponent damage by 2, so an early kill against you is nearly impossible (base 1 + Vengeful = 2, still under the kill threshold of 3). By the time you fire, events have piled up and your shot is likely lethal. Against an Aggressive Rush (pace 2, Vengeful + Seppuku), this build wins **85% of the time**.

**Weaknesses:** guess wrong on dodge and you lose the initiative edge. If your opponent also plays late, the duel becomes a coin flip.

### The Calc Gambit

**Goal:** maximise pistol-round power and accept the risk.

- Fire at pace 1
- Dodge wherever you expect the return shot, since it costs nothing
- Vengeful for Tactics
- Seppuku for Blades (+20% hit chance, +1 damage)

**Strengths:** 70% hit chance (50% base + 20% Seppuku) at pace 1, with 3 damage (1 base + 1 Vengeful + 1 Seppuku). This is the **only non-event combination that reaches the kill threshold at pace 1**.

**Weaknesses:** a dodge at pace 1 makes your shot miss entirely, and Seppuku auto-loses the blades round. With 55.7% of duels reaching blades, that's a heavy liability: players who run this predictably get punished hard by dodge.

### The Mind Reader

**Goal:** counter your opponent's predictable strategy.

- Dodge at the pace you expect them to fire
- Fire at a pace that avoids their likely dodge
- Choose Tactics and Blades to counter their expected picks

**Strengths:** correct dodge timing is the single biggest swing factor in the game, +47.6% win rate. Devastating against opponents who run the same strategy every time.

**Weaknesses:** it lives and dies on the read. Guess wrong and you've built your whole strategy on a misread.

## Key Strategic Principles

**Initiative vs. lethality.** This is the fundamental tension in every duel. Fire first and you can't be pre-empted, but you probably won't kill. Fire later and your damage climbs, but you might be dead before you get the chance. There's no universally correct answer: it depends on your cards, your read on your opponent, and your appetite for risk.

**The blades tax.** More than half of duels reach blades. Any strategy that ignores the blades round is ignoring the most common way duels end. Seppuku's pistol-round power is real, but its automatic blades loss is a steep price when most duels get there.

**Dodge as punishment.** Dodge doesn't just protect you; it punishes predictability. An opponent who runs the same strategy every duel is an opponent you can dodge. They waste their shot, and if they picked Seppuku, they've guaranteed their own loss. The threat of dodge is what keeps the metagame honest.

**Event deck asymmetry.** The Environment deck deliberately carries more positive cards (+damage, +hit chance) than negative ones. The longer a duel runs, the higher the chance, not the guarantee, that accumulated events push your stats into lethal territory. A naked shot at pace 1 has roughly 4% kill probability; by pace 10 it's about 40% (events only, no Tactics or Blades). The game rewards patience with power, but your opponent accumulates the same advantage, and if they fire first and kill you, your stored-up power never gets spent.

**Read your opponent, not just the numbers.** The tables define the landscape, but Pistols at Dawn is ultimately a game of people. What did your opponent do last duel? What do they do when they're losing, or confident? The social deduction layer is where the real depth lives.
